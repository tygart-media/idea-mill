# Estimate vs Statute — Insurance Short-Pay Checker

> Upload a carrier estimate or damage photo set; get back the missing line items and the specific statutory hook that justifies an appraisal demand or supplement.

## The document

The carrier's estimate PDF or the set of damage photos taken the same day. Fields extracted: line items, labor hours, procedures listed, photo density/angles/scale. No login for the first answer. Pick one surface first: collision or residential storm.

## The public file

The 2026 state appraisal/claims-handling statutes — the "file" here is the law itself:

1. **Texas SB 458** (new Insurance Code Chapter 1813) — for personal auto and residential property policies delivered/issued/renewed on/after Jan 1, 2026: binding appraisal provision for amount-of-loss disputes; either side can demand unilaterally; award binding except fraud/accident/material mistake. Implementing rules (proposed 28 TAC §§5.9800–5.9806) set demand windows and deadlines.
2. **Washington WAC 284-30-390** (effective Oct 18, 2026) — minimum claims-handling duties; carriers can't condition coverage on photo-only evaluation; process language for stalled supplements.
3. **Illinois** — its own amount-of-loss appraisal path added in the same window. Florida floated mandatory human review of denials; Oregon issued virtual-adjustment guidance. Direction of travel: virtual allowed, pure automated denial/lowball getting harder.

## The join

Estimate (line items + labor hours) or photo set (density, angles, scale) → short-pay pattern checklist (missing labor hours, OEM procedures, secondary damage under proper light, scale problems virtual adjusters under-count) → the specific statutory hook per state (TX Ch. 1813 appraisal demand, WA WAC 284-30-390 supplement language) → numbered flags with the citation that makes each flag matter.

## The verdict

- **Short-pay flagged** — missing line items / density patterns supporting higher hours, each tied to a statutory hook; draft the appraisal demand or supplement, human sends.
- **Estimate looks complete** — no systematic gaps found against the checklist (inferred).
- **Photo set too thin** — sparse photos can't support the checklist; the essay's core finding is that sparse sets become lowball scopes, so this verdict includes what to re-shoot (inferred from essay).

## Guardrails

- Appraisal demands, supplements, and formal disputes are irreversible — the model drafts the demand letter, photo index, and line-item comparison; a named human owns the send.
- Shops cannot file the appraisal demand themselves (Texas) — they build the file and coach the customer; the product must not pretend otherwise.
- Never act as counsel of record; never auto-file disputes.
- The product is not "AI for claims adjusters" — it's the vertical combination of regulated document/photo review plus trades claim recovery.

## Limitations

- Statutes are state-specific and new (TX Jan 2026, WA Oct 2026) — the checklist must be jurisdiction-tagged; a Texas demand template doesn't work in Washington (stated in essay).
- The appraisal right is the policyholder's/insurer's, not the shop's — the product coaches, it doesn't file (stated in essay).
- Carriers change their virtual-adjustment models; under-count patterns drift — the checklist needs the labeled corpus to stay current (stated in essay).
- Photo-set quality is the input bottleneck: the whole thesis is that dense, angled, scaled sets get paid and sparse sets get lowballed — garbage in, garbage verdict (stated in essay).
- Start one surface (collision OR residential storm); the carrier/ZIP/procedure short-pay map needs hundreds of closed files before it's a moat (stated in essay).
